## Summary

The classic quantitative examination of whether monetary shocks + sticky prices can account for the central RER facts: high volatility (std ~3× GDP) and high persistence (autocorr ~0.83). With sufficient stickiness and high risk aversion the model matches volatility, but generates **too little persistence** (the "persistence anomaly"), and requires separable preferences that conflict with other evidence. Also highlights the consumption–RER anomaly (Backus-Smith).

## Relevance to NK-IOSOE

- Establishes that the standard worry in this literature is RER persistence being too LOW in sticky-price models — a persistent model TCR is the opposite of the classic failure mode.
- Benchmark for reporting model std(Q) and autocorr(Q) against data (our SMM moment 14).
